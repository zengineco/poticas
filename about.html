About This Site & Editorial Standards

Who makes this, what the rules are, and every correction we've ever published — in public, where corrections belong.

Who runs Poticas.com

Poticas.com is made by Vincent Gonzalez, an independent builder and researcher. It started the way most potica stories start: with a grandmother, a floured cloth, and a recipe that lived in her hands. This site exists so that what she and a hundred thousand bakers like her knew doesn't depend on memory alone.

Vincent also contributes potica and povitica history to Wikipedia and is preparing a research paper on the pastry's American record. The front door for questions, corrections, and recipes is the community page.

The standards

  • Nothing here is staged. The community feed contains only real posts by real people. No seeded content, no fake likes, no invented testimonials — ever. When we found seeded content in our own database, we deleted it.
  • Facts trace to sources. Historical and legal claims (Trubar, Valvasor, the EU TSG specification, the diaspora record) cite institutional and press sources, listed on each page. Our reference backbone is About Potica.
  • We only claim what we've read. No citation goes on this site — or into our Wikipedia contributions — that we haven't personally opened.
  • Affiliate links are labeled. Some shop links earn us a commission (disclosed on the shop section); recommendations that earn nothing are labeled that way too, because some of the best sources aren't affiliates.
  • Corrections are published, below, permanently. A reference site that hides its fixes isn't a reference site.

Corrections log

4 July 2026

Trubar dating. We previously stated potica was first recorded in Primož Trubar's 1550 Catechismus. The 1550 book is the first printed in Slovenian, but the pastry's first written mentions are in Trubar's catechism writings of 1575 and 1577 (per Slovenia.si). Corrected across the site and in our Wikipedia contributions.

4 July 2026

TSG filling list. Several pages listed poppy seed among the five fillings protected by EU Regulation 2021/656. The protected list is walnut, walnut with raisins, raisin, tarragon, and tarragon with cottage cheese. Poppy seed potica is deeply traditional — but it is not on the TSG list, and we said it was. Fixed everywhere.

3 July 2026

"UNESCO" claim. We described potica's 2021 heritage recognition as UNESCO status. It is an entry in Slovenia's national register of intangible cultural heritage (December 2021) — a national honor, not a UNESCO listing. Corrected.

Spot an error we haven't? Tell us in the community — corrections earn our sincere thanks and a permanent line on this page.
