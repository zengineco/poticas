Chocolate Potica

By Poticas.com  ·  Updated August 2026

Not one of the old protected fillings — and nobody in Slovenia minds. Chocolate potica is the modern crowd-pleaser, usually a chocolate-walnut hybrid. Here is how it is built.

What is chocolate potica?

A modern variant of potica in which cocoa or melted dark chocolate is worked into the filling — most often blended with ground walnuts rather than used alone, so the roll keeps the structure of the classic. It is not among the fillings protected under the EU "Slovenska potica" name, but it has become one of the most popular contemporary versions, especially with children and at Christmas.

A Modern Addition

Chocolate potica is worth being honest about: it is not one of the traditional protected fillings. The fillings recognized under the EU "Slovenska potica" specification are walnut, walnut with raisins, raisin, tarragon, and tarragon with cottage cheese. Chocolate is a newer arrival — a contemporary favorite that rode in with cheap, good chocolate in the twentieth century.

That does not make it a lesser potica. It makes it a living one. Slovenian bakers add chocolate freely, and it is often the version children ask for. The most successful chocolate poticas are not pure chocolate at all — they are chocolate-walnut, using the nut to keep the body and bite of a real potica.

The Chocolate-Walnut Filling

Per one loaf of the standard dough, a reliable chocolate-walnut filling:

  • 250 g walnuts, finely ground
  • 50 g cocoa powder, or 100 g dark chocolate (50–70%), melted
  • 120 g sugar
  • 100 ml warm milk
  • 50 g butter
  • 1 egg
  • 1 tsp vanilla, pinch of salt, optional pinch of cinnamon

Method: warm the milk and butter, stir in the cocoa or melted chocolate and the sugar, then pour over the ground walnuts. Beat in the egg and vanilla to a thick, spreadable paste. It should hold a soft peak, not run — if it is loose, add a spoonful more ground walnut.

Pure Chocolate vs Chocolate-Walnut

Two roads, both driven:

  • Chocolate-walnut (above) holds the layers, slices cleanly, and tastes like a potica that met chocolate — the version most worth making.
  • Pure chocolate, sometimes using a chocolate-hazelnut spread as a shortcut, is softer, sweeter, and easier, but the filling is looser and can leak or slump. Chill it before spreading and do not overfill.

Rolling and Baking

Roll, prove, and bake exactly as for the walnut version — rolled thin, about 175 °C for roughly 50 minutes, then cooled fully before slicing. Two chocolate-specific cautions:

  • Chill a loose filling first. Melted chocolate spreads better when slightly set; a warm, runny filling tears thin dough.
  • Watch the bottom. The extra sugar and cocoa can scorch — bake on a light-colored sheet and check the underside near the end.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is chocolate potica traditional?

No — it is a modern variant. The fillings protected under the EU Slovenska potica name are walnut, walnut with raisins, raisin, tarragon, and tarragon with cottage cheese. Chocolate is a contemporary favorite rather than a protected traditional filling, though it is widely baked and loved in Slovenia today.

Can I use Nutella for chocolate potica?

Yes, as a shortcut. A chocolate-hazelnut spread makes an easy filling, but it is looser and sweeter than a walnut-anchored one and can leak or slump in the oven. Chill it before spreading, do not overfill, and expect a softer slice than the classic.

What chocolate is best for chocolate potica?

Dark chocolate in the 50 to 70 percent range balances the sweet dough without turning cloying. Use cocoa powder instead if you want a drier, firmer filling that slices more cleanly; use melted chocolate for a richer, glossier one.

What is the difference between chocolate potica and babka?

They are cousins. Babka is the Ashkenazi Jewish chocolate-swirled enriched bread, usually made with a thicker dough and fewer, fatter swirls. Chocolate potica rolls the dough much thinner and carries more spiral layers, and it is typically anchored with ground walnut rather than being pure chocolate.
