Classic Walnut Potica (Orehova Potica)

By Poticas.com  ·  Updated July 2026

The definitive filling of the definitive pastry: ground walnuts, honey, rum, and a dough rolled thin enough to spiral a dozen times per slice.

How do you make walnut potica?

Make an enriched yeast dough (500 g flour, warm milk, butter, egg yolks), prove one hour, and roll it to about 3 mm on a floured cloth. Spread with ground toasted walnuts mixed with warm honey, rum, and whipped egg whites, roll tightly, prove 45 minutes, and bake at 175 °C for 50–55 minutes. Cool completely before slicing.

Before You Start

Walnut potica is the version Slovenians consider definitive — walnut is the first of the five fillings approved for the EU-protected "Slovenska potica" designation, and it is the one most diaspora families carried to America. This recipe makes one large loaf in a standard loaf tin or a 22–24 cm ring mould (potičnik). If you want the background on the pastry itself, start with the full guide to potica.

At a Glance

  • Yield: one large potica, about 16 slices
  • Active time: roughly 1 hour
  • Total time: about 4 hours, plus cooling (which is mandatory — see Rule 3)
  • Difficulty: intermediate — the rolling is the skill, not the dough
  • Equipment: loaf tin or ring mould, rolling pin, a clean cotton cloth for rolling

Ingredients

The Dough

  • 500 g plain flour, plus extra for dusting
  • 7 g instant dry yeast (one standard sachet)
  • 2 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 250 ml warm whole milk (warm to the touch, not hot — about 37 °C)
  • 60 g unsalted butter, melted and cooled
  • 2 egg yolks
  • Zest of one lemon

The Walnut Filling

  • 500 g walnuts, toasted and finely ground — texture matters enormously; here is how to get it right
  • 150 g honey, gently warmed until pourable (not hot)
  • 2 tbsp dark rum
  • Zest of one lemon
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon
  • Pinch of ground clove
  • 2 egg whites, whisked to stiff peaks

Method, Step by Step

1. Make the dough

Combine the flour, yeast, sugar, and salt in a large bowl. Make a well in the centre and add the warm milk, melted butter, egg yolks, and lemon zest. Bring it together and knead until smooth and slightly tacky — about 10 minutes by hand, 6–7 in a stand mixer. Cover and prove somewhere warm for about 1 hour, until doubled. (New to enriched doughs? The dough guide explains what "doubled" and "proved" actually look like.)

2. Make the filling while the dough proves

Toast the walnuts for 8 minutes at 160 °C, cool them completely, then grind them fine. Mix the ground walnuts with the warmed honey, rum, lemon zest, cinnamon, and clove. Fold in the stiffly whisked egg whites last — they lighten the filling so it stays moist and spreadable instead of setting into a dense paste during the long bake.

3. Roll the dough thin

On a well-floured cloth, roll the proved dough to a rectangle roughly 50 × 35 cm and about 3 mm thick. This step has its own technique — the cloth, the resting, the direction you roll — and it is the step that decides whether your slices show tight spirals or air gaps. Read the rolling guide before your first attempt.

4. Fill, roll, and seal

Spread the filling evenly, leaving a 2 cm border on three sides and 4 cm along the far long edge for the seam. Roll tightly from the near long edge, using the cloth to lift and guide. Pinch the seam closed along its full length, tuck the ends under, and place the roll seam-down in a greased tin. Prove another 45 minutes.

5. Bake

Bake at 175 °C for 50–55 minutes. If the top is browning too fast, tent it loosely with foil after 30 minutes. The loaf is done when the internal temperature reaches about 90 °C, or a skewer pushed to the centre comes out without wet dough on it.

6. Cool — completely

Turn it out of the tin and cool it on a rack to room temperature before slicing. A warm potica falls apart at the knife; a cool one slices into clean spiral rounds. This is the hardest step and it is not optional.

The Three Rules

  • The rum is not decoration. It keeps the walnut filling moist through nearly an hour in the oven. If you cannot use alcohol, substitute 2 tbsp of milk with a squeeze of lemon — but do not simply omit the liquid.
  • Warm the honey, never cook it. Honey that gets hot turns the filling runny, and a runny filling slides during rolling and leaks during baking.
  • Cold before the knife. Potica actually improves overnight — the filling settles into the crumb. If you can bake it the day before you need it, do. More on keeping it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I make walnut potica without rum?

Yes — replace the 2 tbsp of rum with 2 tbsp of milk plus a small squeeze of lemon juice. The point of the liquid is to keep the ground-walnut filling moist through a long bake, so substitute it, don't skip it.

Can I use a food processor to grind the walnuts?

Yes, with care: work in batches and pulse rather than running it continuously, or the walnuts release their oil and turn into paste. You want a texture like coarse damp sand. See the walnut grinding guide for the full technique.

Why did my potica have air gaps between the layers?

Almost always a loose roll or an over-proved second rise. Roll tighter than feels polite, and don't let the shaped loaf prove much past 45 minutes. The troubleshooting guide covers the other causes.

Can I bake potica in a regular loaf pan?

Yes. The traditional pan is the ring-shaped potičnik, but the loaf tin is the standard in most American diaspora kitchens and produces an excellent potica. See the pan guide for sizing.
