How Many Calories Are in Potica?

By Poticas.com  ·  Updated August 2026

There is no official number — potica is a homemade pastry with a hundred recipes. But the ingredients tell a clear story, and a reasonable estimate for a slice of walnut potica lands around 250 to 350 calories.

How many calories are in a slice of potica?

Roughly 250 to 350 calories for a typical slice of walnut potica, driven mostly by the walnuts, butter, and sugar. This is an ingredient-based estimate, not an official figure — the real number depends entirely on the recipe and how thick you cut it. A thin slice of a lean recipe can be under 200; a generous slice of a rich, butter-heavy one can top 400.

The Honest Estimate

Let us be straight about this up front: there is no official calorie count for potica. It is a homemade pastry with countless family recipes, no standard slice size, and no nutrition label. Anyone quoting a single exact number is guessing with false precision.

What we can do is reason from the ingredients. Potica is calorie-dense because its two defining components — ground walnuts and an enriched butter-and-egg dough — are both rich. Walnuts alone run about 650 calories per 100 grams. Add butter, sugar, and eggs, and a filling-heavy slice adds up quickly.

A typical slice — call it 70 to 90 grams, a finger’s width — lands in the range of 250 to 350 calories for the classic walnut version. Treat that as a sensible middle, not a fact.

How It Varies by Filling

The filling moves the number more than anything else. Rough per-slice estimates, same caveats applying:

Chocolate-walnut~280–380 kcalAdded chocolate and sugar
Cheese (skuta)~200–300 kcalLighter, more water, less nut fat
Apricot / fruit~200–290 kcalFruit is lighter than nuts, if not over-sugared

These are estimates built from typical ingredient amounts. Your recipe, your slice thickness, and how heavy a hand you have with butter and sugar will shift every one of them.

How to Get Your Real Number

If you want an actual figure rather than a range, the only honest way is to compute it from your own recipe:

  1. Add up the calories of every ingredient in the whole potica (flour, walnuts, butter, sugar, eggs, milk — each package lists its own).
  2. Count how many slices the finished roll gives.
  3. Divide the total by the number of slices.

A kitchen scale makes this far more accurate, since baking is measured by weight. It is the difference between a guess and a number you can trust.

So, Is Potica “Healthy”?

Potica is a celebration food, and it is honest about being one. It is rich in calories, but the bulk of them in the classic version come from walnuts — which bring real nutrition: unsaturated fats, plant protein, fiber, and minerals. It is not a diet food, and it was never meant to be. It is a slice-with-coffee, holiday-table pastry, and the right amount is a slice.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories are in a slice of potica?

Roughly 250 to 350 for a typical slice of walnut potica. It is an ingredient-based estimate, not an official figure — a thin slice of a lean recipe can be under 200, and a generous slice of a rich one can top 400.

Why is there no exact calorie count for potica?

Because potica is a homemade pastry with countless recipes, no standard slice size, and no nutrition label. Any single exact number is false precision. The only accurate figure is one you calculate from your own recipe and slice count.

Is potica fattening?

It is calorie-dense, mostly from walnuts, butter, and sugar. That makes it a rich treat rather than an everyday food. In the classic walnut version, much of the calorie load comes from the nuts, which also bring good fats, protein, and fiber.

Which potica has the fewest calories?

Generally the lighter-filling versions — cheese (skuta) or a not-over-sugared fruit filling like apricot — since they carry more water and less nut fat than the walnut or chocolate rolls. Slice thickness still matters more than most people expect.
