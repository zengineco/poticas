Whole poppy seeds taste like almost nothing

They are sealed capsules — baked whole, they stay gritty and bland. Ground, they release their oils and become the dark, fragrant paste the pastry is named for. If a poppy seed roll ever disappointed you, this step was probably skipped. Poppy seed potica →

The cookbook written for readers with no grandmother left to ask

Woman's Glory — The Kitchen, published by the Slovenian Women's Union of America for its silver anniversary (1926–1951), was compiled — in editor Albina Novak's words — as an "instructor" for women who wished to make "dishes for which their mothers have always been famous." 6 August 2026

The name is the instruction

Potica comes from the Slovenian verb poviti — to wrap, to roll up. The older form povitica contracted as the pastry became Slovenia's national dish. Everything the pastry is — thin dough, filling to the edges, the tight spiral — lives inside that one verb. Etymology →

Same pastry, older name

Kansas City's Croatian community never shortened the word: there it is povitica, and the Strawberry Hill Baking Company has made it commercially since 1984 — a fourth generation of the family bakes it today. In Slovenia the same word wore down to potica. The naming story →

On Minnesota's Iron Range, three miners' foods crossed every ethnic line and became the region's shared table: potica (Slovenian), pasties (Cornish), and porketta (Italian). 26 July 2026

The Iron Range's potica institution was founded by a Roman

Sunrise Bakery in Hibbing, Minnesota, was founded in 1913 by Giulio Forti, a scratch baker from Rome. Potica entered the family in 1932, when Forti's son married the daughter of Croatian immigrants — and a 1991 Los Angeles Times mention later set the mail-order phones ringing. The Iron Range's intermarried food history, in one bakery. Potica in America →

Potica is better on day two — plan for it

Fresh from the oven is the wrong day to judge a potica. Overnight, moisture migrates from the filling into the crumb and the flavours knit. Bakers deliberately bake a day ahead. If your schedule allows the rest day, take it. Storing guide →

1689: the first full description

Janez Vajkard Valvasor's encyclopedic Glory of the Duchy of Carniola (1689) gave the first detailed description of potica — thin dough coated with honey and walnuts, tightly rolled — a recipe that mirrors the modern walnut potica almost exactly, three and a half centuries later. More →

4 July 2026

Potica's first appearance in print was a complaint

The earliest written record of potica comes from Primož Trubar's catechism writings of 1575 and 1577 — the reformer who gave Slovenian its first printed book also disapproved, in print, of women offering potice as votive gifts. The pastry was already so embedded in folk life that it made it into theology. More →

4 July 2026

Only five fillings are legally 'Slovenska potica'

Under EU Regulation 2021/656, a potica sold as protected "Slovenska potica" may contain exactly five fillings: walnut, walnut with raisins, raisin, tarragon, or tarragon with cottage cheese. Everything else is delicious — but it isn't the protected article. The full specification →
